resumo: "We were up early to be at the Spitzkoppe Nature Park reception by 7:30 for a guided hike — described to us as easy, which turned out to be a generous assessment. Three and a half kilometres, roughly three hours, with steep climbs. What surprised us was how rewarding it was before we even reached the top: partway up, the landscape opened up in a way that stopped us in our tracks — an expansive view over the plains that was, on its own, worth the climb. The summit of the Pontok Mountains delivered even more. From up there, zebras were visible grazing on the plain far below, small and unhurried in the distance.

The descent was just as steep as the ascent, and required its own concentration. On the way down, our guide brought us to within close range of two zebras — much more immediate than the distant silhouettes from the summit — before leading us to the Small Bushmen Paradise. The paintings there are few and small, easily overlooked at first glance. But they are between two and four thousand years old, left by the San people on rock that has been standing here for over a hundred million years. That combination of scales — human and geological — gives the place a particular stillness.

Back at the cabin tents, we made lunch on the communal grills and let the afternoon pass quietly. At five, we set out for the Rock Arch — a natural opening in the granite shaped like an eye, oriented to frame both sunrise and sunset. We climbed the rock beside it as the sun descended, and watched southern Africa go dark from above. Then back to camp, a fire we built ourselves, and an evening spent around it under a sky dense with stars."
